## Make your evaluation and funding decision
- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The 4% individual-to-team conversion rate. At 3,000 weekly-active individuals, 4% produces 120 team conversions and, at 6 seats per team, 720 paid seats / $103,680 annualized revenue. If conversion is 25% lower at 3%, this falls to 90 teams, 540 seats, and $77,760 annualized revenue -a 25% reduction.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The model applies the 4% conversion rate to all 3,000 weekly-active individuals, even though the target customer is specifically SMB wellness leads. It does not show what share of those 3,000 users are actually in a position to convert a workplace team. This makes the projected 120 team conversions and $103,680 annualized revenue structurally uncertain.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** No. It defines a measurable threshold and timing is below 3% conversion by the end of Q2, but the consequence is only to “reassess the go-to-market approach.” That sends the decision back to the room. A complete kill criterion would state that if conversion remains below 3% by the end of Q2, Q3 headcount will not be committed and further GTM expansion will be paused.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** FUND WITH ONE CONDITION: validate that the 4% conversion rate holds within the actual target population of SMB wellness leads, rather than across the full 3,000 weekly-active user base, before committing Q3 headcount.

## Write your business case
- **The strategic bet. What specific outcome are you backing, who does it serve, and what is the mechanism that connects the product decision to a financial result?:** Redesign Fable’s onboarding to establish an evolving wellbeing model from Day 1, helping users move from immediate stress relief toward progressively building, maintaining, and evolving sustainable wellbeing habits.

The bet is that introducing this progression early will make Fable relevant as users’ needs change, increasing sustained engagement beyond 90 days and reducing the point at which users feel they no longer need the product.

Value mechanism: evolving wellbeing habits → sustained engagement → lower post-90-day churn → higher Premium renewal → longer customer lifetime → increased subscription revenue.
- **The assumptions. List the assumptions your case rests on, then rank them: which one, if wrong, most changes your conclusion?:** 1. Most critical: Users who come to Fable for acute stress relief have an ongoing need for wellbeing support and are willing to continue using Fable as their needs evolve.

2. Introducing evolving wellbeing habits from Day 1 will materially increase sustained engagement beyond the current 90–120 day acute-use period.

3. Users will adopt progressively evolving routines rather than continuing to use only the meditation, sleep, and breathing experiences that initially brought them to Fable.

4. Increased sustained engagement will translate into lower post-90-day churn and higher Premium renewal, creating incremental subscription value.

5. The initiative can be delivered and validated incrementally by the existing 14-person cross-functional team, limiting investment exposure before the core assumptions are proven.
- **The expected return. What does the bet generate and when? Express it at unit level (per customer) and at scale (what volume hits target).:** At the unit level, the initiative creates value by extending the retained lifetime of existing Fable users. Each incremental Premium user retained beyond the post-acute phase generates additional subscription contribution margin without requiring Fable to replace that lost subscriber.
At scale, the initiative is designed to contribute to:
- 20% reduction in post-90-day churn (KR1)
- 40% of post-90-day users engaging at least 3 days per week (KR2)
- 15% increase in Premium renewal among post-acute users (KR3)
Fable has demonstrated scale of up to 4.2 million active users, so even modest improvements in post-acute retention could affect a meaningful user population. However, 4.2M is a historical peak and should not be treated as the current eligible cohort in the financial forecast.

The financial return will therefore be measured as:
Incremental contribution margin from additional Premium renewals and extended customer lifetime − incremental initiative cost.
Before full-scale rollout, Finance will populate the model with the current eligible cohort, Premium price, contribution margin, baseline post-90-day churn and renewal rate, and incremental initiative cost.
Full-scale investment is approved only if the validated economics meet Fable’s required LTV:CAC and payback thresholds. Because those financial thresholds and inputs are not provided in the case, the business case does not invent them.
- **The kill criterion. Name the specific metric, threshold, timeline, and financial consequence that tells the team to stop. Actionable, not a conversation.:** Investment is released progressively; each phase must earn the next phase of investment.
Phase 1 
Journey Adoption: Within 4 weeks of releasing the redesigned onboarding, the test cohort must demonstrate a positive lift in adoption of the evolving wellbeing journey versus the existing onboarding control. If it does not, pause the initiative and do not fund Phase 2 until the approach is revised and revalidated.

Phase 2 
Habit Formation: Within 8 weeks of releasing the evolving-habits experience, the cohort must demonstrate progress toward 40% of users engaging at least 3 days per week (KR2). If it does not, pause expansion and do not commit additional product or engineering capacity.

Phase 3 
Retention & Renewal: Once the first eligible cohort reaches Day 90, the initiative must demonstrate progress toward a 20% reduction in post-90-day churn (KR1) and a 15% increase in Premium renewal (KR3) versus baseline/control. If it does not, stop further investment and do not fund full-scale rollout.

Financial gate: Even if the product KRs are achieved, Fable does not scale the initiative unless Finance confirms that incremental contribution margin and customer lifetime economics meet the company's required LTV:CAC and payback thresholds.
